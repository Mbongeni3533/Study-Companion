# A-Z of Web Research

A short course in finding, judging and citing sources online. Work through the
videos first, then use the Purdue OWL sections as reference while you write.

## Start with technique

1. **How to skim internet articles** - a practical method for deciding within a
   minute whether a source is worth reading.
   <https://youtu.be/iuCRDeUYXww>

2. **Evaluating and validating research sources** - how to tell a credible
   source from a confident one.
   <https://youtu.be/FKaH7PlXx1Q>

## Plan the research

3. **Research: where to begin** - choosing a topic, narrowing it and building a
   research question before you search.
   <https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/conducting_research/research_overview/index.html>

4. **Conducting primary research** - interviews, surveys, observation and
   experiments, and when each is appropriate.
   <https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/conducting_research/conducting_primary_research/index.html>

## Judge what you find

5. **Evaluating sources of information** - bibliographic evaluation, reading
   evaluation, print versus internet sources and evaluating web sources.
   <https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/conducting_research/evaluating_sources_of_information/index.html>

6. **Web research** - search engines, Boolean operators, web directories and the
   invisible web, with an annotated links section.
   <https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/conducting_research/searching_online/index.html>

7. **Internet references** - dictionaries, style manuals, grammar handbooks,
   editing resources, indexes for writers, online libraries and e-texts,
   newspapers, journals and magazines.
   <https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/conducting_research/internet_references/index.html>

## Work in the archives

8. **Archival research** - conducting research in different kinds of archives,
   with the considerations and best practice for each.
   <https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/conducting_research/archival_research/index.html>

## Before you cite a source, check

- Who wrote it, and what are their qualifications?
- Is it primary evidence, reporting or opinion?
- When was it published or last updated?
- Does it cite sources you can follow?
- Is it published by a university, government, journal or recognised institution?
